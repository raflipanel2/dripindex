FIELD GUIDE 04 / LEAK DETECTION

Why a toilet keeps running after the flush

Distinguish an occasional refill from continuous tank filling without guessing at replacement parts.

By DripIndex EditorialPublished & reviewed 11 Nov 2026Educational field note

The phrase “running toilet” can describe several different behaviors: a short normal refill after flushing, a tank that never stops filling, or a quiet refill that returns every few minutes. Those patterns point to different checks. Before buying a part, capture which behavior actually happens, because a single photo of the tank rarely answers the question.

What to notice first

Before changing equipment, record the timing and conditions of the observation. These are clues worth investigating, not proof of a particular fault.

  • A steady stream continues long after the expected refill period.
  • The fill valve activates for short bursts between flushes.
  • Water visibly enters the overflow tube continuously.

A controlled check

Use the same setting, timing and measurement method whenever possible. Stop if a test would require unsafe access, pressurized disassembly or exposure to electricity.

  1. 01

    Time how long filling continues after a normal flush. Compare with the usual behavior of this exact fixture rather than a universal number.

  2. 02

    Look into the tank from a safe position without touching moving mechanisms. Note whether water rises to the overflow tube and whether the float seems to stop filling.

  3. 03

    Use the dye test in a separate quiet interval to check for tank-to-bowl seepage. Keep these observations distinct in your notes.

  4. 04

    Report the pattern, approximate refill timing and any visible defects to a plumber or property manager before replacing parts. Shut off water only if you know the intended local fixture shutoff works freely.

How to interpret what you find

Continuous filling near the overflow may indicate a fill-level or fill-valve issue. Repeated brief refills can be consistent with water escaping past a seal. But these are clues, not diagnoses; fixture designs vary. If the toilet is overflow-prone or loose at its base, address the immediate water risk before performing further tests.

EXAMPLE / FIELD RECORD

Field note: toilet starts refilling about every eight minutes, no one flushed, and a dye test later colors the bowl. That is stronger evidence of a tank-to-bowl leak than the sound alone. Send both observations so a technician can choose the next check efficiently.

What to check next

If you are deciding between documenting the refill and arranging a repair immediately, use the scale of the problem to prioritize. Continuous overflow into the bowl can add up even when it does not wet the floor; water over the rim or onto the floor is more urgent. A household member may be able to tell you whether intermittent refilling happens only after a particular flush button is used. Keep the observation non-invasive and avoid assuming a particular replacement kit is compatible with your model.

Pitfalls and safety boundaries

Do not bend plastic mechanisms blindly or overtighten connectors. Stop testing if the floor is wet or there is evidence of sewage backup.

If the source is hidden, a fixture is failing, or moisture continues, contact a qualified plumber or your property manager. Protect occupants from slip, electrical and contamination risks. This article cannot inspect your individual plumbing system.
