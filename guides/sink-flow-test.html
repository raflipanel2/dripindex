FIELD GUIDE 13 / MEASUREMENT

A 15-second sink flow test you can repeat

Measure tap output at a known setting and compare before-versus-after changes.

By DripIndex EditorialPublished & reviewed 11 Nov 2026Educational field note

A faucet’s flow can be useful to know when comparing fixtures or investigating unexpected splashing. A short bucket test is more reliable than calling a tap “high pressure” based on feel, but the result depends on handle position and collection accuracy. Record both so the number is comparable when you test again.

What to notice first

Before changing equipment, record the timing and conditions of the observation. These are clues worth investigating, not proof of a particular fault.

  • An older tap has a stronger stream than you remember.
  • You want to compare two bathroom sinks before buying equipment.
  • A nozzle change seems to affect filling speed, but you have not measured it.

A controlled check

Use the same setting, timing and measurement method whenever possible. Stop if a test would require unsafe access, pressurized disassembly or exposure to electricity.

  1. 01

    Use a container with measured volume markings, positioned so the entire stream lands inside it. Dry the area to avoid slipping.

  2. 02

    Set the handle to a repeatable position. Collect water for 15 seconds and measure the volume in liters.

  3. 03

    Multiply liters by four to estimate liters per minute, or use the Fixture Flow Bench with your actual collection duration.

  4. 04

    Repeat three times. A large spread suggests inconsistent handle setting or missed water rather than a precise fixture rate.

How to interpret what you find

Collecting 1.5 liters in 15 seconds implies 6 liters per minute. A 2-minute continuous use at that setting would be about 12 liters, but real handwashing normally involves intermittently switching the tap off. Flow is only one variable in household water consumption.

EXAMPLE / FIELD RECORD

Field note: baseline samples 1.5, 1.4 and 1.5 L/15 seconds. After a manufacturer-approved outlet change: 1.0, 1.1 and 1.0 L/15 seconds. The comparison suggests lower measured output at that handle position.

What to check next

If you plan to compare a product before and after cleaning, take the baseline at a fixed faucet opening and with the same container. A poorly calibrated jug can introduce more uncertainty than the flow change you are trying to measure. Repeat the test several times and compare the range, not just the highest number. If the faucet is equipped with an automatic sensor or flow limiter, look up its manufacturer behavior; it may deliver a variable stream that invalidates a simple constant-flow assumption.

Pitfalls and safety boundaries

Do not handle very hot water, block drains or force an adapter onto incompatible threads. Water quality and pressure adequacy are separate questions.

If the source is hidden, a fixture is failing, or moisture continues, contact a qualified plumber or your property manager. Protect occupants from slip, electrical and contamination risks. This article cannot inspect your individual plumbing system.
