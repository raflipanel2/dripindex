FIELD GUIDE 08 / MOISTURE

Under-sink moisture: a safe observation checklist

Track drips, condensation and stored-item damage before opening plumbing connections.

By DripIndex EditorialPublished & reviewed 11 Nov 2026Educational field note

Water under a sink can come from a supply connector, drain trap, seal around the basin, a dishwasher hose or condensation. The location of a puddle alone does not identify the source because water can travel along surfaces. A careful observation checklist helps maintenance find the cause without risky trial-and-error repairs.

What to notice first

Before changing equipment, record the timing and conditions of the observation. These are clues worth investigating, not proof of a particular fault.

  • Cabinet base feels damp or material begins to swell.
  • Wetness appears mainly after the sink drains, rather than when the tap first turns on.
  • A connector looks wet even when the sink has not been used recently.

A controlled check

Use the same setting, timing and measurement method whenever possible. Stop if a test would require unsafe access, pressurized disassembly or exposure to electricity.

  1. 01

    Remove only loose stored objects and photograph the moisture pattern. Avoid contact with electrical outlets or appliances in the cabinet.

  2. 02

    Dry accessible surfaces safely, then observe when water reappears: during tap use, during draining or without any intentional use.

  3. 03

    Place a clean dry paper towel beneath a suspected area only if it is safe and accessible; note the first point that becomes wet. Do not loosen fittings during the test.

  4. 04

    If moisture persists or materials are soft, report it promptly with photos and a simple timeline. Ask a plumber to inspect concealed connections and damaged cabinet components.

How to interpret what you find

Water arriving during draining may suggest a drain-side issue, whereas wetness during idle periods may warrant checking a supply-side line. Neither distinction is conclusive. Persistent moisture also matters because porous cabinet material may stay wet after the visible puddle disappears. Drying and addressing the source should happen promptly.

EXAMPLE / FIELD RECORD

Field note: dry at 8 AM, droplets reappeared near the rear pipe opening after dishwasher cycle at noon, no sink use. This narrows the timing but still requires safe professional inspection; do not assume the nearest pipe is the culprit.

What to check next

If the cabinet looks dry at the next inspection, examine whether stored items show earlier exposure—warped cardboard, a water line on the cabinet wall or discoloration near the back corner. Such evidence can show that a leak has occurred intermittently. In contrast, water only after heavy sink splashing may be entering from the counter seam. Do not test by deliberately overflowing the sink; use normal operation and photos. Persistent cabinet swelling often requires assessment beyond a surface wipe.

Pitfalls and safety boundaries

Never use an electrical appliance near a wet outlet. Do not mix cleaners under the sink or disturb a pressurized fitting. If water is actively spraying, prioritize safe isolation by a competent person.

If the source is hidden, a fixture is failing, or moisture continues, contact a qualified plumber or your property manager. Protect occupants from slip, electrical and contamination risks. This article cannot inspect your individual plumbing system.
